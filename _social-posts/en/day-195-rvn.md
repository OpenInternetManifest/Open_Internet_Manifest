  ### RVN: 1 percent for whoever exposes the fraud
  
  Yesterday in the Oval Office.  
  Nick Shirley shakes Trump’s hand.  
  Vance stands next to him.  
  Trump thanks him for the videos about the Minnesota daycare centers.
  
  Then the announcement.  
  Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program.  
  Anyone can submit tips via fraud.gov.  
  Trump says: whoever exposes fraud that leads to recovery gets 1% of the amount.  
  No limit.  
  He calls himself the arbiter.
  
  The Justice Department site says something else.  
  Up to $30,000 if the tip leads to charges.  
  Treasury is putting $25 million aside.  
  The difference between the president’s words and the website is right there.
  
  ### What Shirley did
  
  His videos from December 2025 showed empty buildings that were being funded as active daycare centers.  https://openinternetmanifest.org/social-posts/en/day-09-rvn.html
  Minnesota received federal money for them.  
  One center — Quality Learing Center — later closed.  
  A woman from the videos pleaded guilty to wire fraud.  
  Shirley now receives $10,000 as the first payout of the program.
  
  ### The point
  
  A citizen with a camera and a YouTube channel exposed something the existing controls had not stopped.  
  The response is a reward system.  
  1% sounds large.  
  The formal rules are smaller and conditional.  
  Both are in the open.
  
  Whether the program structurally reduces fraud, or mainly generates tips that are later filtered, has not yet been measured.  
  What has been measured: Shirley’s videos led to investigations, closures, and at least one conviction.
  
  **Question for you**
  
  If a YouTuber gets $10,000 for footage that was already online…  
  what does that say about the existing controls?

### RVN: 1 percent for whoever exposes the fraud

Yesterday in the Oval Office.  
Nick Shirley shakes Trump’s hand.  
Vance stands next to him.  
Trump thanks him for the videos about the Minnesota daycare centers.

Then the announcement.  
Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program.  
Anyone can submit tips via fraud.gov.  
Trump says: whoever exposes fraud that leads to recovery gets 1% of the amount.  
No limit.  
He calls himself the arbiter.

The Justice Department site says something else.  
Up to $30,000 if the tip leads to charges.  
Treasury is putting $25 million aside.  
The difference between the president’s words and the website is right there.

### What Shirley did

His videos from December 2025 showed empty buildings that were being funded as active daycare centers.  https://openinternetmanifest.org/social-posts/en/day-09-rvn.html
Minnesota received federal money for them.  
One center — Quality Learing Center — later closed.  
A woman from the videos pleaded guilty to wire fraud.  
Shirley now receives $10,000 as the first payout of the program.

### The point

A citizen with a camera and a YouTube channel exposed something the existing controls had not stopped.  
The response is a reward system.  
1% sounds large.  
The formal rules are smaller and conditional.  
Both are in the open.

Whether the program structurally reduces fraud, or mainly generates tips that are later filtered, has not yet been measured.  
What has been measured: Shirley’s videos led to investigations, closures, and at least one conviction.

**Question for you**

If a YouTuber gets $10,000 for footage that was already online…  
what does that say about the existing controls?
