  ### RVN: Red Line Amsterdam – the demonstration and the banners
  
  On Sunday 8 November 2026, organisers estimated that around 200,000 people joined the fourth Red Line demonstration in Amsterdam. They wore red, waved Palestinian flags and demanded that the Jetten government sever all economic and military ties with Israel. Police described the demonstration as having proceeded “without major incidents”. Four people were arrested for relatively minor offences.
  
  Along the route, including near the Overtoom, masked people held banners reading “We support Hamas”. The same type of banner had been seen earlier at a demonstration on Dam Square on 7 October. The organisers (Amnesty, Oxfam Novib, Save the Children, Pax and others) said they could not fully rule out that Hamas supporters would join, but stressed that the march was about human rights. NOS reporters also heard participants chanting that they would “never be for Hamas”.
  
  A frequently heard argument for these expressions is that they stem from “the genocide” by Israel in Gaza. The war in Gaza is extraordinarily cruel. That is not in dispute. The question is whether the term “genocide” fits.
  
  ### What was already there
  
  Hamas is not a neutral or abstract term.
  
  - The organisation has been on the EU terrorist list since 2003 (Common Position 2001/931/CFSP). Its military wing had been on the list since 2001. Since January 2024 the EU has had a separate sanctions regime against individuals and entities that support or enable violent actions by Hamas and Palestinian Islamic Jihad.
  - On 7 October 2023, Hamas and other armed Palestinian groups carried out a coordinated attack on Israel. According to Israeli figures and cross-checks by outlets including AFP and Human Rights Watch, approximately 1,195 people were killed (the majority civilians) and 251 people were taken hostage. The attack included targeted shootings of civilians, hostage-taking, arson and sexual violence. Human Rights Watch and others have classified these acts as war crimes and crimes against humanity.
  
  Genocide has a strict legal definition. Article II of the 1948 Genocide Convention refers to acts committed with the intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial or religious group as such. The International Court of Justice (ICJ) has issued provisional measures in the South Africa v. Israel case (January, March and May 2024), but has not yet delivered a final judgment on genocide. The proceedings continue; the next written round is scheduled for 2027.
  
  A UN commission of inquiry concluded in September 2025 that Israel had committed genocide. That commission does not speak for the UN as a whole and is not a judicial body. Against that conclusion stand concrete elements that often receive less attention in reporting:
  
  - Israel has repeatedly warned civilians via leaflets, text messages, phone calls and “roof knocks”.
  - Evacuation corridors and zones have been designated.
  - The border with Egypt has not been completely sealed. Tens of thousands of Gazans have left via Egypt (figures from OCHA and Egyptian authorities vary, but the outflow is documented).
  
  These facts do not rule out cruelty, civilian deaths or possible war crimes. They do make it harder to prove the specific genocidal intent required by the Convention. The war began after the 7 October 2023 attack, which targeted the killing and hostage-taking of civilians.
  
  The presence of “We support Hamas” banners is not an isolated incident. Earlier demonstrations in Amsterdam (including on 7 October 2026) showed the same texts. Mayor Halsema then called them “shocking and distasteful”, but stated that they were not punishable at the time.
  
  ### The pattern
  
  The organisers distance themselves. The banners are still there. The large turnout (organisers’ estimates: 100,000, 150,000, 250,000 and now 200,000 across successive editions) is widely presented as a moral signal. Open support for an organisation that is on the EU terrorist list and responsible for the murder and hostage-taking of civilians on 7 October 2023 is often treated in reporting as “fringe” or “not representative”.
  
  The argument “it comes from the genocide” treats the term as an established fact. Its legal status is not that: there is no final ICJ judgment, and there are concrete measures (warnings, evacuations, partial outflow via Egypt) that cut against classic genocidal patterns. This is a factual observation, not a judgment on the legitimacy of criticism of Israeli policy.

### RVN: Red Line Amsterdam – the demonstration and the banners

On Sunday 8 November 2026, organisers estimated that around 200,000 people joined the fourth Red Line demonstration in Amsterdam. They wore red, waved Palestinian flags and demanded that the Jetten government sever all economic and military ties with Israel. Police described the demonstration as having proceeded “without major incidents”. Four people were arrested for relatively minor offences.

Along the route, including near the Overtoom, masked people held banners reading “We support Hamas”. The same type of banner had been seen earlier at a demonstration on Dam Square on 7 October. The organisers (Amnesty, Oxfam Novib, Save the Children, Pax and others) said they could not fully rule out that Hamas supporters would join, but stressed that the march was about human rights. NOS reporters also heard participants chanting that they would “never be for Hamas”.

A frequently heard argument for these expressions is that they stem from “the genocide” by Israel in Gaza. The war in Gaza is extraordinarily cruel. That is not in dispute. The question is whether the term “genocide” fits.

### What was already there

Hamas is not a neutral or abstract term.

- The organisation has been on the EU terrorist list since 2003 (Common Position 2001/931/CFSP). Its military wing had been on the list since 2001. Since January 2024 the EU has had a separate sanctions regime against individuals and entities that support or enable violent actions by Hamas and Palestinian Islamic Jihad.
- On 7 October 2023, Hamas and other armed Palestinian groups carried out a coordinated attack on Israel. According to Israeli figures and cross-checks by outlets including AFP and Human Rights Watch, approximately 1,195 people were killed (the majority civilians) and 251 people were taken hostage. The attack included targeted shootings of civilians, hostage-taking, arson and sexual violence. Human Rights Watch and others have classified these acts as war crimes and crimes against humanity.

Genocide has a strict legal definition. Article II of the 1948 Genocide Convention refers to acts committed with the intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial or religious group as such. The International Court of Justice (ICJ) has issued provisional measures in the South Africa v. Israel case (January, March and May 2024), but has not yet delivered a final judgment on genocide. The proceedings continue; the next written round is scheduled for 2027.

A UN commission of inquiry concluded in September 2025 that Israel had committed genocide. That commission does not speak for the UN as a whole and is not a judicial body. Against that conclusion stand concrete elements that often receive less attention in reporting:

- Israel has repeatedly warned civilians via leaflets, text messages, phone calls and “roof knocks”.
- Evacuation corridors and zones have been designated.
- The border with Egypt has not been completely sealed. Tens of thousands of Gazans have left via Egypt (figures from OCHA and Egyptian authorities vary, but the outflow is documented).

These facts do not rule out cruelty, civilian deaths or possible war crimes. They do make it harder to prove the specific genocidal intent required by the Convention. The war began after the 7 October 2023 attack, which targeted the killing and hostage-taking of civilians.

The presence of “We support Hamas” banners is not an isolated incident. Earlier demonstrations in Amsterdam (including on 7 October 2026) showed the same texts. Mayor Halsema then called them “shocking and distasteful”, but stated that they were not punishable at the time.

### The pattern

The organisers distance themselves. The banners are still there. The large turnout (organisers’ estimates: 100,000, 150,000, 250,000 and now 200,000 across successive editions) is widely presented as a moral signal. Open support for an organisation that is on the EU terrorist list and responsible for the murder and hostage-taking of civilians on 7 October 2023 is often treated in reporting as “fringe” or “not representative”.

The argument “it comes from the genocide” treats the term as an established fact. Its legal status is not that: there is no final ICJ judgment, and there are concrete measures (warnings, evacuations, partial outflow via Egypt) that cut against classic genocidal patterns. This is a factual observation, not a judgment on the legitimacy of criticism of Israeli policy.
