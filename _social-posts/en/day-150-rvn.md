### RVN: 1st Amendment Auditors – what we can learn from this

For five days we looked at 1st Amendment Auditors.  
What they do, why it is allowed, which cases exist, and how stark the contrast with the Netherlands and Europe is.

Now the closing question: what can we learn from this, and how do we build digital sovereignty?

### The lesson

The American auditors show something simple.  
Power that may not be filmed is power that does not have to answer for itself.  
A camera in public space is not a provocation. It is a basic form of accountability.

In the Netherlands and Europe that cultural reflex is weaker. The legal space is smaller and greyer. Officials and officers more often react defensively. Publishing images carries more risks. Yet the principle remains: anyone who wants to check power must be able to record what happens.

### The real point

Filming itself is not the problem.  
The problem is who controls the images and the data.

As long as that data sits on closed platforms or with central parties, control remains limited. Open source, own infrastructure and verifiable systems change that. Then you decide what happens with the data, not a gatekeeper.

### What we can do

We do not have to copy American auditors.  
We can take the core and translate it to our own context.

That means:
- Documenting what happens in public space, even when it is uncomfortable.
- Building parallel structures that do not depend on the goodwill of platforms or governments.
- Creating verifiable systems: own servers, own AI, hash verification, open protocols.

That is exactly what the Open Internet Manifest is working on.  
Nexus, local AI, clean hashes, own infrastructure.  
Not because we hate existing systems, but because systems that cannot be checked will sooner or later ignore their own rules.

### The way out

Not only complaining about selective enforcement or disappearing records.  
Building alternatives that can actually be verified.

The auditor’s camera is a physical mirror.  
Digital sovereignty is the digital mirror.  
Both belong to a society that takes itself seriously.

**Question for you**

What is the most important lesson from this series for you?  
And what will you concretely do to become less dependent on black boxes?
