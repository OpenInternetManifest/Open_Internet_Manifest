### RVN: Ilhan Omar – the van at the door

A few weeks ago: who she is, the questions, the papers https://openinternetmanifest.org/social-posts/en/day-151-rvn.html.

Today: Homeland Security Investigations at her home near Washington. Early in the morning. A judicial warrant, obtained by the Justice Department. Boxes going out. Part of an ongoing federal criminal investigation.

No arrest. No indictment. A van, though.

### What was already on the table

Part 1 noted three things.

An ICE investigation into possible immigration fraud, or a recent one.  
A FOIA refusal with the standard phrase about “enforcement proceedings.”  
A swing in the financial disclosures: first millions via her husband’s winery, later revised down to a maximum of about $125,000. An accounting error, the office said.

The marriage to Ahmed Nur Said Elmi (2009–2017) remained what it was. Allegation: her brother, to keep him in the country. Denial. No DNA, no ruling.

### What is added

Fox writes, citing a source, that the search concerns immigration paperwork her late father filed when she was a child. Justice confirms the warrant and the investigation. Not the subject.

Omar: abuse of power. Intimidation of a political opponent. She has done nothing wrong. Congress is in recess. She is running for reelection.

A warrant is not a conviction. A refusal citing “enforcement proceedings” is no longer an empty form either. That sentence now has an address.

### The split

Weaponization is the shop window. Every search of an opponent gets that word.  
A judge who signs is the till. He does not sign on a tweet.

Whether the file holds up is for the investigation to show. Until then two sentences stand side by side. She: nothing done. Justice: ongoing criminal investigation, boxes in the van.

### OIM way out

Do not run ahead of the charge. There isn’t one.  
Do not turn the search into theatre because the person is well known.

Do: warrant, agency, what comes out of it.  
The FOIA sentence from part 1 and the van from part 2 belong in the same piece.

**Question for you**

If a refusal says “enforcement proceedings” and a search follows…  
which do you believe first, the denial or the warrant?
