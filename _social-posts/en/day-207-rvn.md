### RVN: Russell Brand – the conviction and the silence

In November 2026, Russell Brand was sentenced in the United Kingdom to 10 years and 6 months in prison for rape and multiple sexual assaults. The judge described the offences as serious and the impact on the victims as significant. Brand denied the allegations until the end.

At the same time, attention for the grooming gangs – groups that abused girls for years in places including Rotherham, Rochdale and Oxford – remains limited, despite the scale and the official reports that have existed since 2014.

### What was already there

The Jay Report (2014) concluded that in Rotherham alone approximately 1,400 girls were victims of organised sexual exploitation between 1997 and 2013. The perpetrators were predominantly of Pakistani heritage. Later reports in other towns confirmed a similar pattern. Prosecutions and convictions followed, yet national media attention remained relatively limited for years.

Brand was first publicly accused in 2023-2024. In November 2026 the conviction followed after a trial. The facts in his case are now judicially established.

### The difference in attention

Brand’s conviction received immediate and extensive coverage in British and international media. The grooming gang cases, despite their scale and the earlier reports, never received comparable, sustained public attention. The contrast is not an opinion: it is visible in the volume and duration of the reporting.

### What this means

Brand’s conviction is a fact. The scale of the grooming gangs according to the official reports is also a fact. The difference in media attention is documentable.

The pattern that stands out is not “who is guilty”, but “where the attention goes and where it stays away”. Both cases concern sexual abuse. Only the scale and the length of the silence differ.
