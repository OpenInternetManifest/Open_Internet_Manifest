### RVN: The emergency stock goes to market

The headline: Trump can be satisfied. The Netherlands puts 753,000 barrels of diesel and petrol up for sale.

The cabinet: not a rush job for Trump. Third tranche. Already pledged. On schedule.

Two sentences. One tank.

### What is on the table

50 kilotons of diesel. 45 kilotons of petrol. About 753,000 barrels, from the strategic stock held by COVA. No kerosene. That stays in reserve.

Within the IEA action the Netherlands had pledged 5.4 million barrels, after supply from the Middle East fell away. With this tranche, the cabinet says, almost 4.7 million have been released. About 87 percent of the pledge.

Van Veldhoven: on schedule. The legal stock of ninety days is still there.

The pump is not. Fuel is pushing inflation. Excise duty down: no reason, the cabinet says.

### The translation

First the frame. There is a war in the Middle East. Supply has fallen away. Europe depends on diesel from outside. The pump is expensive. The cabinet says so itself.

Then the measure. A slice of the emergency stock goes to auction. Officially to fill that gap and temper the price. That is the sentence of the IEA action.

Then the scale. 753,000 barrels is two tanks. The Netherlands uses that in days, not in a season. On the world market it is a line in a table. An auction of this size does not press the price at the pump. At most the story that “something is being done”.

Then the closing line. We are on schedule. 87 percent of the pledge is gone. Kerosene stays. Diesel and petrol do not. The ninety-day norm remains on paper.

Three steps. Scarcity. Sale. Reassurance.

Groningen was closed under the same kind of sentence. Safety. Then imports. Now the tank. Auction. Then “we still have ninety days”.

Ninety days is a norm. Not a full tank for a longer quarrel. And a buffer you auction to press a price it does not press is no longer a reserve. It is a gesture.

### OIM way out

Do not turn every auction into a sell-off.  
Do not take “on schedule” for the outcome.

Do: how much was there. How much is gone. What comes back, and when.  
Do: kerosene stays, diesel and petrol go. For whom.

An emergency stock that goes to market is no longer an emergency. It is policy.

**Question for you**

If the pump is expensive and the reserve goes to auction…  
what does that stock still catch if things really go wrong?
