RVN: Three stories, one pattern

In Utrecht a 20-year-old man was found dead in a freezer. In The Hague a 23-year-old man hit a police officer on the head after stopping his car in the middle of the road. And via Zembla images appeared of illegal cage fights at which FVD members were present. Three different days, three different stories. Yet the same pattern: a short story that spreads quickly, and documents that arrive later.

Utrecht – the freezer

On Tuesday 24 November 2026 a 15-year-old boy found the body of his brother Mohammed in a freezer on Timmerstraat. Mohammed, 20 years old, a Syrian refugee, struggled with addiction and mental health problems. He was known to Buurtteam Ondiep. On Sunday night he had knocked on the door. “Dad, let me in, I’m cold.” The father did not let him in, according to the family on the advice of the care providers.

The family says he climbed into the freezer himself to shelter or sleep and could not get out. The police are keeping all scenarios open and are not treating it as a crime for the time being. Mayor Dijksma has announced her own investigation into the circumstances and the guidance.

The Hague – Stationsweg

Saturday 7 November 2026, around 15:30, Stationsweg. A 23-year-old man (Iyad) stopped his white Mercedes in the middle of the carriageway. He says he stood still “for less than 20 seconds” to load a cake. A cycling police officer addresses him. According to the police he did not respond, refused to show his ID and did not want to get out. A woman (according to him his mother) intervenes. The officer pushes her aside. Immediately afterwards Iyad gets out and hits the officer several times on the head. With reinforcement he is handcuffed. He has been arrested for assaulting a police officer, insult, resistance and failing to show ID.

His frame: the officer came at him aggressively right away and pushed his mother. He only intervened to protect her. The longer footage shows that the car was standing on the carriageway and that he refused to cooperate before the blows were struck.

The cage fights

Zembla showed images of illegal cage fights in Finsterwolde, Zuidwolde and Oeffelt, among other places. Participants and spectators gave the Hitler salute. Members of the JFVD fought. Ramon van Asch (FVD Den Bosch) was present at a fight in Oeffelt. FVD eventually expelled six members.

Lidewij de Vos said at the party congress and in interviews that there is no place for violence or involvement with violent organisations, that she viewed the images with disgust, and that members who associate themselves with it should resign their membership. She stated that the party was unaware of the gatherings and that she now wants to move on to the order of the day. The media framing is mainly “not hard enough” or “she does not want to talk about it”. Police and the Public Prosecution Service are investigating the images.

What is the same

In all three cases a story arrived quickly:

  • Utrecht: “tragic accident, he climbed in himself.”
  • The Hague: “I did nothing, the officer was aggressive.”
  • Cage fights: “FVD = neo-Nazis” or “she has not spoken out hard enough.”

In all three cases there are documents that do not automatically confirm or close that story:

  • Utrecht: the police are keeping all scenarios open, the care providers had advised keeping him outside.
  • The Hague: the car was standing on the carriageway, he refused ID and refused to get out.
  • Cage fights: the party has expelled members and spoken out against violence. You cannot control where members go, but you can speak out and take action.

The difference in treatment becomes visible when compared with the murder of Fortuyn. At the time the left-wing corner (PvdA, GroenLinks, parts of the media) was broadly held responsible for the demonisation. Those same groups subsequently denied or minimised any concrete link with Volkert van der Graaf. With the Zembla images FVD is broadly held responsible, even though members have been expelled and statements have been made, and that denial or minimisation is absent.

OIM way out

Do not treat one story as the end.
Do not treat the other story as proof.

Instead: what the police in Utrecht are actually investigating and what Buurtteam advised.
What is visible on the longer footage of Stationsweg before the first blow.
What is precisely visible on the Zembla images, what Lidewij de Vos said, and what police and the Public Prosecution Service are investigating on the basis of that.

Verifiable documents and the sequence of events above a story that quickly calls it “tragic”, “aggressive officer” or “not hard enough” and moves on.

Question for you

How often have you seen three different stories…
in which the narrative arrived faster than the question of what exactly preceded it?
