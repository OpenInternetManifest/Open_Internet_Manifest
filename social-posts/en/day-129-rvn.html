RVN: Fauci’s Diary – The Underreported Evidence

Everyone is talking about Fauci invoking the Fifth Amendment more than 100 times.
Very few are talking about what is actually in his own diary.

Senator Rand Paul released more than 1,100 pages of Fauci’s personal notes.
This document is one of the most underestimated pieces of the entire COVID dossier.
It doesn’t just show what Fauci was thinking — it shows how large the gap was between his private reality and the public narrative.

1. Lab-leak: private vs public

As early as January 2020, Fauci writes in his diary that the Wuhan market was likely not the source, but merely the amplifier.
He notes conversations with scientists, some of whom thought the virus looked “constructed.”

At the same time, he publicly dismissed the lab-leak theory for a long time as a conspiracy theory.

2. Gain-of-function and the move

In 2014 the Obama administration placed a moratorium on risky gain-of-function research inside the United States, partly because of previous lab incidents.
Shortly afterward, similar research continued via EcoHealth Alliance in Wuhan.
Fauci’s institute played a central role in the funding.

3. The prizes and the misuse of federal staff

During the pandemic Fauci received large sums of money, including the Dan David Prize of nearly $900,000.
Documents show that federal NIH employees (paid with taxpayer money) wrote the nominations for him, handled the ethics procedures, and coordinated the payments.

Internal emails included lines such as:

“Congratulations….you’re rich!” and “I want my 15%.”

This is not a minor detail.
Using government employees for personal enrichment and prestige is, at the very least, a serious integrity violation.

4. The pattern

Privately: doubt, strategic thinking, focus on status and money.
Publicly: absolute certainty, “I am the science,” criticism = an attack on science itself.

OIM way forward

It is a primary source that shows how narrative and reality can diverge — and how power, image, and personal interests can merge.

Remaining silent under oath is one thing.
But what someone wrote privately for years while telling the public something else is at least as revealing.
