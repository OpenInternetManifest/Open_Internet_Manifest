RVN: The buildup – planes, ships, cloud and fridges

Friday, 13 November 2026. American air traffic came to a standstill.

The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop for all departing flights. Cause: a communication issue affecting the Air Route Traffic Control Centers. Switches and routers became overloaded and went down. It lasted more than four hours. Thousands of flights were delayed or cancelled. It was the longest nationwide stop since 9/11.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy pointed to the age of the system. Homeland Security said there was no indication of a cyberattack.

That same day in New Orleans, the tanker Atlantic Asphalt collided with the docked river cruise ship American Heritage. The cruise ship capsized. More than 180 people were on board. All were rescued, 32 taken to hospital, none with critical injuries. The Coast Guard said the tanker lost steering capability. The investigation is ongoing.

At the same time, Microsoft 365 was down. Outlook, Teams and Copilot were unavailable or severely disrupted in 74 countries. The outage started on Thursday and lasted more than 24 hours. Microsoft called it a technical issue caused by elevated load on infrastructure following an earlier network capacity upgrade. The company said it saw no indications of a cyberattack.

Earlier this month there had already been targeted cyberattacks on supermarket refrigeration systems in multiple European countries. Temperatures were remotely raised to 50 °C. Food had to be thrown away. Similar attacks had previously hit France. No perpetrator identified.

What is actually on the record

The FAA outage has been attributed to outdated hardware that became overloaded.
The collision in New Orleans is a physical accident whose precise cause is still under investigation.
The Microsoft outage has been characterized by the company itself as a technical cause.
The supermarket attacks are cyber and target the food chain.

None of the official statements link these events to each other.

The pattern

Four visible disruptions in a short time:
air traffic, shipping, worldwide cloud services and food refrigeration.

Three of them currently have a technical or accidental explanation.
One is clearly malicious and has been repeated.

The clustering in time is striking. A common actor or cause has not been demonstrated.

What this means

The FAA outage shows what happens when a central system becomes outdated and overloaded.
The Microsoft outage shows what happens when millions of people and organizations depend on a single gatekeeper.
The supermarket attacks show that even physical systems controlled via the internet become vulnerable once that connection is centralised or poorly secured.

In every case the same principle applies: whoever hands over control also hands over responsibility. Until it fails.

OIM way out

This is why we are building a free and open internet without gatekeepers.

Not taking the one framing (“grand conspiracy”) as the end of the story.
Not taking the other framing (“just bad luck”) as the end of the story.

Instead:
the official FAA statements,
the ongoing investigation into the New Orleans collision,
Microsoft’s own incident reports,
and the earlier reporting on the European supermarket attacks.

Primary records and the order in which they became available above a story that only shows the clustering.
Own nodes, own data, cryptographic verification and systems you can control yourself — above dependence on central platforms that determine your route.

Question for you

How often have you seen multiple, separately explained disruptions coincide in a short time…
without a common cause or actor later emerging?
